1. A starting point for curiosity

Animal Generator is a free tool for drawing prompts, writing ideas, classroom activities and casual games. Its names, photographs and filter labels are provided for general information and inspiration.

The site is not a species-identification service or a substitute for scientific, veterinary, animal-care, wildlife-safety or conservation advice. Do not use a random result to decide how to approach, handle, feed or care for an animal.

2. Animal information has limits

We review the catalog and link to references, but mistakes and outdated information are possible. Names and taxonomy can change, and habitat, diet, adult size and native range can vary by population, life stage or source.

Filters are broad editorial summaries designed to help discovery. They are not a complete description of a species or a scientific classification system. See Animal data sources for definitions, measurements, references and species-specific limitations. Verify facts against reliable, current sources before relying on them in teaching, publication or other work.

A reviewed photograph is a visual reference. It may show one individual in captivity or another particular setting; it does not establish the species’ usual habitat, behavior, size or wild distribution.

3. What a random result means

The generator selects from the website’s eligible catalog: species with enabled, reviewed photos that match your chosen filters. It does not sample every animal species on Earth or reflect how common an animal is in nature.

The no-repeat feature prioritizes unseen species while matches remain and begins another round when needed. Your filters, previous picks and available photos affect which animals can appear. These results are intended for informal activities, not certified draws, scientific sampling or decisions that require independently verifiable randomness.

4. Photo credits and reuse

Animal photographs come from Wikimedia Commons and iNaturalist. Each photograph retains its own creator, source and license, recorded on the Animal photo credits page. We do not claim ownership of third-party photographs.

Using this website for free does not give every photo the same reuse permissions. Before downloading, republishing, adapting or using a photo commercially, check its source page and exact license. Depending on the license, you may need to credit the creator, link to the license, identify changes or share an adapted image under the same license. Public-domain and CC0 material have different terms.

A generated animal name or an idea for your own drawing does not transfer ownership of the reference photo. Nothing on this page replaces the license attached to a particular image.

6. Corrections and copyright concerns

If an animal fact, photo identification, credit or license looks wrong, please report it through the project’s issue tracker or the contact page. Include the animal name, page URL, relevant source and a short explanation so we can investigate.

For a copyright concern, identify the photograph, the original work and the attribution or permission issue. Keep private information out of public issues. Use a private contact option shown on the contact page when your report contains personal information.
